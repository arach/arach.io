### Key Points:

- **Wisdom Can't Be Taught**: Siddhartha meets the Buddha but chooses to find his own path, convinced that enlightenment can't be passed on through doctrine.
- **Experience Over Teaching**: His journey through asceticism, wealth, love and despair shows understanding emerging from lived experience.
- **The River**: The ferryman Vasudeva teaches him to listen to the river, a symbol of time, unity and the simultaneity of all things.
- **Unity of Opposites**: The novel ends with the insight that every truth contains its opposite and that everything is part of a single whole.

### Summary:

"Siddhartha" by Hermann Hesse, published in 1922, follows a young man in ancient India who leaves his Brahmin family to seek spiritual fulfillment. He lives as a wandering ascetic, meets Gotama the Buddha, then turns to the world of the courtesan Kamala and the merchant Kamaswami, where he gains wealth and loses himself.

Disillusioned, Siddhartha comes to a river and becomes a ferryman alongside Vasudeva. Through listening, loss and the love of his son, he finally reaches peace. Hesse's short, lyrical novel is a meditation on self-discovery and the limits of teaching, and it has become a lasting classic.
