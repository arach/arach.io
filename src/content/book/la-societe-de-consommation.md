### Key Points:

- **Consumption as a Code**: People don't consume objects for their use but as signs that mark status and difference within a social system.
- **The Myth of Abundance**: Growth and affluence don't end scarcity; they produce new needs and new inequalities, structurally built into the system.
- **Needs Are Produced**: Needs aren't natural drives that products satisfy; the system of production generates them.
- **The Body and Leisure**: Shows how the body, leisure time and even "personalization" become objects of consumption and social control.

### Summary:

"La société de consommation: ses mythes, ses structures" by Jean Baudrillard, published in 1970, is a sociological critique of postwar affluence. Baudrillard argues that consumption has become the central organizing logic of modern society: a language of signs through which people classify themselves and others, rather than a simple exchange of goods for needs.

The book dismantles the promise that abundance brings equality and happiness, showing instead how consumer society perpetuates distinction and integrates individuals into a system of managed desire. It anticipates Baudrillard's later work on simulation and remains a foundational text for understanding advertising, branding and media culture.
