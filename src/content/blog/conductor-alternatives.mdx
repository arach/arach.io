A few people have asked me for help navigating this, so here is a summary of the research I've done and shared with them. If there's anything you'd like me to discuss, or anything I missed, DM me on [X](https://x.com/arach).

I've spent the past few months on agent-to-agent tools, and I keep comparison sheets on these products as I go. This post folds them together. I checked each claim below against the maker's own pages on November 4, and listing a tool is not an endorsement.

## Majors and up-and-comers

I sort these into two groups. The split is my judgment, not a measured ranking. Cloud means the maker runs agents on its own computers; every tool here also has a desktop, phone or web client. Remote means you can reach your own other machines, such as a server or a box over SSH. Free means a plan you can keep using without paying; a trial doesn't count. License is the open-source license in the public repository, and a dash means there is no public repository. A dot means the tool's own pages back it, as of November 4; a blank means they didn't say. Stars are from GitHub on the same day, rounded.

<ConductorMatrix />

Stars measure attention, not quality, and a hosted tool's paying users never show up in them.

## Where the agents run

Most of these tools have desktop, phone or web clients, and a client doesn't tell you where the agents execute. Each entry below says it: local (your machines), cloud (the maker's computers), or both. Paradev, cmux, Orchestrator and Cloudroom do both. Replicas, Boxes.dev and Superconductor run only on the maker's computers.

## A closer look at the majors

<ToolEntry name="Orca" url="https://www.onorca.dev/" group="Major" runs="Local" price="Free, MIT">

Desktop and mobile clients, worktrees, review, and access to your own remote machines. Remote access here means your machines, not managed compute. [Repository](https://github.com/stablyai/orca).

</ToolEntry>

<ToolEntry name="T3 Code" url="https://t3.codes/" group="Major" runs="Local" price="Free">

A desktop, mobile and web agent interface with review and remote access to your own hosts. Worktrees are covered in its [user docs](https://github.com/pingdotgg/t3code). [Remote access](https://github.com/pingdotgg/t3code/blob/main/docs/user/remote-access.md) can go over your LAN, Tailscale, another VPN, SSH or a public URL. T3 Connect is the easy route; it uses T3's cloud tunnels and a sign-in, and a paid plan raises its machine limit. It is MIT licensed. The repository warns that the project is early.

</ToolEntry>

<ToolEntry name="Paseo" url="https://paseo.sh/docs" group="Major" runs="Local" price="Free; Hub Pro $15 per seat">

Desktop, phone and web clients for daemons running on your machines. The app is free and open source under Apache 2.0. The optional hosted [Hub](https://paseo.sh/hub) manages triggers, not agent compute. Hub Pro is $15 per seat per month, and model and machine costs stay yours.

</ToolEntry>

<ToolEntry name="Paradev" url="https://paradev.com/" group="Major" runs="Local and cloud" price="Free; Pro $20 per user">

Formerly Superset. Local worktrees and a beta cloud. The Free plan covers one user with local workspaces, the desktop app and the CLI. Pro is $20 per user per month and adds remote access, automations and the phone app. Cloud workspaces start on Team, at $60 per user per month with 150 hours per seat. Models cost extra ([pricing](https://paradev.com/pricing)). The pricing page changed on November 4; earlier that day it listed 40 cloud hours on Pro. It documents a path from Conductor for local worktrees and Cloud; see below.

</ToolEntry>

## A closer look at the up-and-comers

<ToolEntry name="cmux" url="https://cmux.com/pricing" group="Up-and-comer" runs="Local and cloud" price="Free app, Pro from $50">

A free Mac terminal workspace, and local agents use your own API keys at no cost. Persistent [cloud VMs](https://cmux.com/docs/cloud) are paid early access. Pro starts at $50 a month, and the pricing page has also shown a $15 Plus tier with one saved cloud VM. On cloud VMs, the agents use your team's own accounts through [CodeRouter](https://cmux.com/docs/coderouter). I couldn't confirm automatic worktrees per task.

</ToolEntry>

<ToolEntry name="Orchestrator" url="https://orchestrator.inc/pricing/" group="Up-and-comer" runs="Local and cloud" price="Free local, Starter $20">

Local worktrees with PR and CI feedback loops. [Hosted execution](https://docs.orchestrator.inc/guides/cloud/) needs an enabled build and account access. I couldn't establish whether cloud includes model costs.

</ToolEntry>

<ToolEntry name="Emdash" url="https://emdash.com/" group="Up-and-comer" runs="Local" price="Free">

Parallel CLI agents on macOS, Windows and Linux, with worktrees, diffs and a PR workflow, locally or over SSH. The stable v1 and the 2.0 waitlist are separate things. [Repository](https://github.com/generalaction/emdash).

</ToolEntry>

<ToolEntry name="Traycer" url="https://docs.traycer.ai/guides/review-agent-changes" group="Up-and-comer" runs="Local" price="Free for individuals">

A desktop workspace with worktrees, review and your own [remote hosts](https://docs.traycer.ai/concepts/hosts/remote-hosts), under an [MIT license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/traycerai/traycer/main/LICENSE). Free already shares tasks with people you invite, and Sync shares a task with the whole team for $20 per user per month ([pricing](https://docs.traycer.ai/account/pricing)). Provider costs are separate. Optional Traycer inference adds 20% and includes no monthly credits, and the automatic judge can spend credits.

Stable [1.5.3](https://github.com/traycerai/traycer/releases/tag/desktop-v1.5.3) shipped on November 3. An [open report](https://github.com/traycerai/traycer/issues/3429) describes memory and queue issues; I didn't reproduce it.

</ToolEntry>

<ToolEntry name="Cloudroom" url="https://www.cloudroom.dev/pricing" group="Up-and-comer" runs="Local and cloud" price="Free; Pro US$50 a month">

Runs agents locally or hosted. Its open-source Core runs on your own Ubuntu 24.04 server, while GPUs, Cloud loops and computer use run only on Cloudroom Cloud ([FAQ](https://www.cloudroom.dev/docs/faq)). The Free plan includes 3,000 cloud credits a month and unlimited local agents; Pro is US$50 a month ([pricing](https://www.cloudroom.dev/pricing)). Compute credits don't cover models. Its local diff and worktree features don't mean the cloud has the same ones ([local threads](https://www.cloudroom.dev/docs/local-threads)).

</ToolEntry>

<ToolEntry name="Replicas" url="https://replicas.dev/" group="Up-and-comer" runs="Cloud" price="Developer $50 a month">

Hosted Linux workspaces. It has desktop and mobile clients, but the agents run on its computers. The plan includes $50 of usage ([billing](https://docs.replicas.dev/admin/billing)), and you bring your own model [credentials](https://docs.replicas.dev/admin/credentials). It documents a [move from Conductor Cloud](https://docs.replicas.dev/migrate/conductor-cloud) for workspaces, setup scripts, secrets, routines and team.

</ToolEntry>

<ToolEntry name="Boxes.dev" url="https://boxes.dev/" group="Up-and-comer" runs="Cloud" price="Starter $19 per seat">

Hosted Linux computers for Claude Code and Codex. Starter includes 50 box-hours and four awake devboxes plus a Template. A standard hour is 4 vCPU and 8 GiB, and extra usage is $0.60 per box-hour. Larger boxes and an awake Template use more, and models cost extra ([billing](https://boxes.dev/help/plans-and-box-hours)).

</ToolEntry>

<ToolEntry name="Superconductor" url="https://www.superconductor.com/docs/implementations" group="Up-and-comer" runs="Cloud" price="Free plan, Pro from $50">

Hosted agent environments and PR review. Pro starts at $50 a month for 20,000 credits and rises with the credits you pick ([docs](https://www.superconductor.com/docs/pricing)); the [pricing page](https://www.superconductor.com/pricing) opens on the $100 tier. The free plan includes 20,000 credits, and compute costs 100 credits an hour at 1 vCPU and 2 GB. Model tokens can come out of credits; bring your own tokens and credits go only to the cloud computers.

The maker [posted](https://x.com/sergeykarayev/status/2118067017878302855) "Free Pro until end of the year for CC customers!", and the pricing page gives no terms for it.

</ToolEntry>

## A shout-out to Amp

[Amp](https://ampcode.com/) plays a different game from the tools above: it is a coding agent with its own development environment. It still answers the same need. A thread can run in an orb, a machine Amp sets up with your code, tools and plugins, so the agent keeps working after you close your laptop ([manual](https://ampcode.com/manual)). It can also run on a runner or in a local CLI session ([threads](https://ampcode.com/docs/threads)). Orbs can be Amp-hosted or self-hosted, and your Mac can serve as a runner. You can start one from your phone and follow up from your Mac or the terminal.

The Hobby plan is free. Amp-hosted orbs are pay-as-you-go. Self-hosted orbs and your own runners are free on every plan except Enterprise. You can bring your ChatGPT or Claude subscription or your own keys, with no Amp token fees. Megawatt is $20 a month with 45,000 minutes on Amp-hosted orbs ([pricing](https://ampcode.com/pricing)).

Amp is also the one tool here that imports Conductor chats. Its co-founder Karl Clement [posted](https://x.com/karlclement/status/2118108530926567430) the steps: update Amp and run `amp threads import conductor --all`, or open Settings, Imports, Import in the Mac app. Move Conductor Cloud chats to your Mac first (right-click, Transfer to local) before November 20. Amp is also offering [three free months of Megawatt](https://ampcode.com/code/AMP-VW9Y-LIHU) to people without a paid Amp plan.

## What moves over

Two tools document a path from Conductor. Paradev can adopt your existing local worktrees, though scripts need converting ([local migration](https://docs.paradev.com/migrate-from-conductor)). Moving off Conductor Cloud means transferring workspaces to local, or pushing branches and recreating the environments ([Cloud migration](https://docs.paradev.com/migrate-from-conductor-cloud)). Archiving an adopted worktree in Conductor can delete its shared folder, so be careful there. [Replicas](https://docs.replicas.dev/migrate/conductor-cloud) moves Conductor Cloud workspaces, setup scripts, secrets, routines and team into its own cloud workspaces. It doesn't take local worktrees.

Only [Amp](https://x.com/karlclement/status/2118108530926567430) imports Conductor chats; see above. Paradev and Replicas both say they can't, and resuming a CLI session depends on compatible saved sessions. [Traycer](https://docs.traycer.ai/first-run) and [Boxes](https://boxes.dev/help/start-and-continue-threads#import-threads-from-your-computer) can import local conversation history from your model provider, but that isn't the same as importing Conductor chats or Cloud exports.
